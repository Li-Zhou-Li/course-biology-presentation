He could see the crosswalk stripes — and count them.

What helped him see again?

A gene from this tiny green algae — the kind you find in any pond.

No brain, no complex thinking — just a simple cell-surface reaction.

The light-sensing process is similar to what’s in our eyes — but human eyes are far more complex.

We don’t rush toward light — we adjust to see better.

So if our eyes are so advanced…

why does this tiny algae protein matter so much for repairing damaged sight?
